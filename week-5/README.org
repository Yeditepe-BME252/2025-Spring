* Relocating a force or a moment
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
Does it make a difference to push or pull something?
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- When a force is moved on it's line of action, the effect produced does not change (for a particle or a rigid body).
- But when that force is moved away from it's line of action, that's a different story.
- And yet a force couple (a pure moment) can be relocated arbitrarily without altering the mechanical system.

** move force on it's LOA
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)

* General Force Systems
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
A rigid body or a system of bodies connected with articulations under a general force system  implies one or more forces and/or couple moments with arbitrary components (not necessarily confined to a particular plane or axis), applied at arbitrary locations (points that are not necessarily lying along a particular axis or plane).
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- These forces can be relocated to a common location by applying the principle of moment about a point for each and every force in the system.
- The relocation of moments does not require any specific operation, they can just be moved.

* Coplanar Force Systems
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
When the forces and motion are constrained to occur within a single plane (such as the gait cycle in the saggital plane, arm/leg abduction in the frontal plane, or twisting the spine by forces in the transverse plane), the problem dimensionality may be reduced from three to two. In that case: 
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- two coordinates will be necessary and sufficient to describe the planar configuration of the mechanical system,
- each force will have only two components residing in that particular plane, and,
- the moments produced by the planar forces will necesarily be perpendicular to this plane.
