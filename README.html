Why does this course exist?

The first course in Biomechanics offered by the Biomedical Engineering Department aims to enhance the understanding of the science of motion and mechanical interactions between living tissue.

Essential reading: Chapter 1 of IB:SFAD/15

  • Point of Departure, the development of biomechanics
  • Health Care Applications, complex mechanical phenomenon in living organisms
  • What Is Continuum Mechanics?
  • A Brief on Cell Biology,
  • The Extracellular Matrix
  • Mechanotransduction in Cells

Syllabus

Course learning objectives-CLOs

  • [X] exemplify some basic biomechanical phenomenon that arise in living tissues ()
  • [X] perform simple algebraic operations with vectors
  • [X] read and verify expressions written in the indicial notation
  • [X] convert expressions between their tensor and indicial forms
  • [X] transform vectors linearly via second-order tensors
  • [X] convert expressions between their tensor and matrix forms
  • [X] perform change of basis of vector components
  • [X] calculate the determinant of a second-order tensor
  • [X] calculate the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a second-order tensor
  • [X] provide examples of scalar and vector fields
  • [X] apply the gradient operator to a scalar field
  • [X] apply the gradient operator to a vector field
  • [X] identify divergence, curl and laplacian operators in cartesian coordinates
  • [X] use density to find continuum mass and center of mass
  • [X] differentiate surface and body forces
  • [X] provide an example to the surface traction vector
  • [X] relate the surface traction vector and the stress tensor
  • [X] provide examples of simple states of stress
  • [X] write the condition of static equilibrium in global form
  • [X] apply the divergence theorem to integral forms
  • [X] obtain the local form of static equilibrium using the divergence theorem
  • [ ] explain the concept of material time derivative
  • [ ] state the balance laws for a system of particles
  • [ ] state the balance laws for a continuum (integral form)
  • [ ] identify mechanical variables in the first law of thermodynamics
  • [ ] state the local (differential) form of balance laws for a continuum

Modus Operandi

Course is composed of a few activities,

  • Lectures
    • Listening and taking notes in the lecture
    • Prepare for next lectures with study questions and openAI
  • Attendence through Yulearn check-in
  • Quizzes
  • Exit Survey Questions
  • Jove Videos
  • Final

Lectures

  • The board, projector, and supplementary materials will be utilized.
  • Asking questions, and taking notes are encouraged.
  • The lecture notes are shared on Yulearn.

Quizzes

Three or four quizzes, half-an-hour with a single question each, will be given during the semester.

Exit Survey Questions

Eight of nine such questions shall be asked to students during the last 5 minutes of lectures during the semester.

Code of Conduct & Ethics

  • The interpersonal communication in class is based on a level of minimum respect towards each other.
  • The class is a communal space, it is not a stage to show off (except for the instructor).
  • Students are expected to arrive to class on a voluntary basis, involuntary participation is strongly discouraged.
  • Mechanics requires a direct teaching model where the role of the student is usually limited to a receiver, however, the students are encouraged to ask questions and participate in the learning process.
  • Here’s a fascinating account of ethics (in Turkish): Etik Değerler ve Eğitimi by Prof. Ioanna Kuçuradi (Maltepe University).
