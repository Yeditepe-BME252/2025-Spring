* ($\S 2.1$) Preliminaries (Session 6)
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- Change in a physical variable, $\phi$, is usually denoted by $d$ or $\partial$, is called a *differential* of $\phi$, such as $d\phi$ or $\partial\phi$.
- The choice of correct representation depends on how many other variables it depends on, e.g. if it depends on a single variable such as $\phi(t)$ then the *differential change in variable* $\phi$ with respect to a *differential change in time*, $dt$, is denoted as $$\frac{d\phi}{dt}$$
- When the physical variable depends on more than one independent variables such as $\phi(x_1, x_2)$, then the *differential change in* $\phi$ with respect to $x_1$ and $x_2$ are denoted as $$\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_1},\;\; \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_2}$$

** ($\S 2.1.1$) Points, Tensors
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- In the remainder of the course a stationary (non-moving) coordinate frame $\{\boldsymbol{e}_i\}$ in Euclidean space $\mathbb{E}^3$ will be used.
- We identify points $\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathbb{E}^3$ with, $$ \boldsymbol{x} = x_i \boldsymbol{e}_i = \begin{Bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{Bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3$$
- We identify vectors $\boldsymbol{v} \in \mathcal{V}$ with, $$ \boldsymbol{v} = v_i \boldsymbol{e}_i = \begin{Bmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \\ v_3 \end{Bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3$$
- We identify second-order tensors $\boldsymbol{S} \in \mathcal{V}^2$ with, $$ \boldsymbol{S} = S_{ij} \boldsymbol{e}_i \otimes \boldsymbol{e}_j= \begin{bmatrix} S_{11} & S_{12} & S_{13} \\ S_{21} & S_{22} & S_{23} \\ S_{31} & S_{32} & S_{33} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3\times 3$$
