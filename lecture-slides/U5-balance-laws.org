* (Chapter 5) Motivation (Session 14)
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- In this final chapter, the fundamental principles of mechanics are laid out.
- First, these ideas are developed for a system of particles.
- Then the concepts of temperature and heat are developed for a continuum body.
- The principles developed for a system of particles are extended to cover a continuum body.
- Using definition of temperature and heat the principle of energy conservation is appended to the prior principles.
- Finally, the local forms of these global principles are developed based on localization.

** ($\S 5.1$) Ideas from Particle Mechanics
#+ATTR_HTML: :width 1200px
[[../images/AFC-5.1.png]]
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- We consider a system of $N$ particles with masses $m_\alpha$ and positions $\boldsymbol{x}_\alpha$ as illustrated above. 
- It will be assumed there is an internal potential regulating the interaction between each particle pair $\alpha-\beta$, which is called $U_{\alpha\beta}$.
- This potential is assumed to be symmetric between particles, i.e.,  $U_{\alpha\beta} =  U_{\beta\alpha}$, and the *interaction force* applied by particle $\beta$ on particle $\alpha$ is given by $$\boldsymbol{f}_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathrm{int}} = -\nabla U_{\alpha\beta}$$
- Furthermore, each particle may be subject to an external (environmental) force, $\boldsymbol{f}_\alpha^{\mathrm{env}}$, and no other forces act on or within the system.

#+REVEAL: split
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- To the configuration $\Omega_t$ of the system of $N$ particles at any time $t\geq 0$, we associate:
  #+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
  + a total mass
    $$M[\Omega_t] = \sum_{\alpha\in N} m_\alpha$$
  + linear momentum
    $$\boldsymbol{l}[\Omega_t] = \sum_{\alpha\in N} m_\alpha \dot{\boldsymbol{x}}_\alpha$$
  + angular momentum
    $$\boldsymbol{j}[\Omega_t] = \sum_{\alpha\in N} \boldsymbol{x}_\alpha \times m_\alpha \dot{\boldsymbol{x}}_\alpha$$
  + internal energy
    $$U[\Omega_t] = \sum_{\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
      \alpha,\beta \in N \\
      \alpha < \beta
    \end{array}} U_{\alpha\beta}$$
  + and kinetic energy
    $$K[\Omega_t] = \sum_{\alpha\in N} \tfrac{1}{2} m_\alpha |\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}_\alpha|^2$$

*** Balance Laws for the $N$ Particle System
From the definitions above we state the fundamental balance principles as
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
1. the mass of $\Omega_t$ does not change with time
   $$ \frac{d}{dt} M[\Omega_t] = 0, $$
2. the rate of change of linear momentum is equal to the resultant external force on $\Omega_t$
   $$ \frac{d}{dt} \boldsymbol{l}[\Omega_t] = \sum_{\alpha \in N} \boldsymbol{f}_\alpha^\mathrm{env}, $$
3. the rate of change of angular momentum is equal to the resultant external torque on $\Omega_t$
   $$ \frac{d}{dt} \boldsymbol{j}[\Omega_t] = \sum_{\alpha \in N} \boldsymbol{x}_\alpha \times \boldsymbol{f}_\alpha^\mathrm{env}, $$
4. and the rate of change of the sum of potential (internal) and kinetic energy is equal to the power of external forces on $\Omega_t$
   $$ \frac{d}{dt}(U[\Omega_t] + K[\Omega_t]) = \sum_{\alpha \in N} \dot{\boldsymbol{x}}_\alpha \cdot \boldsymbol{f}_\alpha^\mathrm{env}. $$

*** Arguments Concerning Temperature and Heat
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- The balance laws for mass, linear momentum and angular momentum can readily be generalized to continuum bodies by replacing the summation by an integral.
- However, balance of energy will require the introduction of two new terms.
- The continuum assumption cannot represent the detail that occurs at length scales comparable to, or shorter than, inter-atomic spacings.
- In particular, the velocity at a point is to be interpreted as the average velocity of atoms in the vicinity of that point.
- Fluctuations in the velocities (vibrations) of atoms about the mean velocity are not represented in the continuum velocity field.
- To account for these fluctuations, we introduce new concepts:
  #+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
  + *temperature*: measure of the size of velocity fluctuations.
  + *heat*: measure of the energy of fluctuations.

** $(\S 5.2.2)$ Thermodynamics
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- We defined the *temperature*, $\theta(\boldsymbol{x},t) > 0$, and *heat* (thermal energy) in a broad sense, now they will be included as actors in mechanical principles.
- Define the net heating as the rate per unit time at which heat is added to $\mathcal{B}$
  $$ Q[\Omega_t] = Q_b[\Omega_t] + Q_s[\Omega_t] $$
- There is a *heat source* (or sink) per unit mass, $r(\boldsymbol{x},t)$, so that,
  $$ Q_b = \int_\mathcal{B} \rho r\, dV.$$
- Also, there exists a heat flux vector field, $\boldsymbol{q}(\boldsymbol{x},t)$, such that its dot product with the unit outward normal to the boundary surface $\partial\mathcal{B}$,  $\boldsymbol{n}$, yield the *heat transfer field* per unit area, $h(\boldsymbol{x},t)$ as:
  $$ h = - \boldsymbol{q} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}$$
- From this the surface heating is found as
  $$ Q_s = \int_{\partial\mathcal{B}} h \,dA.$$
- Thus the net heating will be found as the sum of $Q_b$ and $Q_s$.

*** Energy, Work, Power
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- The Kinetic Energy of body $\mathcal{B}$ is
  $$ K[\Omega_t] = \int_\mathcal{B} $$
- The Power of External Forces is
  $$ \mathcal{P}[\Omega_t] = \int_\mathcal{B} \quad dV + \int_{\partial\mathcal{B}} \quad dA. $$
- By the Net Working, $\mathcal{W}[\Omega_t]$, we mean the mechanical power delivered by external forces that is not used up in producing motion
  $$ \mathcal{W}[\Omega_t] = \mathcal{P}[\Omega_t] - \frac{d}{dt} K[\Omega_t] $$

*** ($\S 5.2.2.3$) Internal Energy and the First Law
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- The energy content not associated with kinetic energy is called *internal energy*, $U[\Omega_t]$.
- In particular, we assume there exists an *integral energy density field* per unit mass $\phi(\boldsymbol{x},t)$ such that
  $$ U[\Omega_t] = \int_\mathcal{B} \rho\phi \, dV. $$

*** [Axiom 5.3] First Law of Thermodynamics
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- The rate of change of internal energy of body $\mathcal{B}$ equals the sum of the net heating and net working applied to it
  $$ \frac{d}{dt} U[\Omega_t] = Q[\Omega_t] + \mathcal{W}[\Omega_t] $$
- This can alternatively be stated in terms of the kinetic energy and power of external forces as
  $$ \frac{d}{dt} (U[\Omega_t] + K[\Omega_t]) = Q[\Omega_t] + \mathcal{P}[\Omega_t] $$

* ($\S 5.3$) Localization of Balance Laws (Session 16)
** ($\S 5.3.1$) Conservation of Mass in Local (Differential) Form
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- The integral statement of the conservation of mass principle
  $$ \frac{d}{dt} \int_{\Omega_t} \rho\,dV = 0, $$
- can be transformed using the *Reynolds Transport Theorem* [Result 4.6] by carrying out the differentiation of the mass integral with respect to time as
  $$ \frac{d}{dt} \int_{\Omega_t} \rho\,dV = \int_{\Omega_t} \underbrace{\dot\rho + \rho (\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{v})}_{\text{0}} dV = 0. $$
- Based on the argument of localization, for the above equation to be true for all subdomains $\Omega_t$ of $\mathcal{B}$, we require $\forall \boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{B},\,t>0$ that
  $$ \dot\rho + \rho (\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{v}) = 0. \quad \mathrm{or} \quad \dot\rho + \rho v_{k,k} = 0 $$
- Sample Problem: By using the indicial notation show that the differential form of mass continuity can alternatively be stated as:
  $$ \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \boldsymbol{v}) = 0 $$

* The First Law of Thermodynamics (Session 17)
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- This is also known as the principle of /conservation of energy/.
- It was defined in global (integral) form and here it will be converted into the local (differential) form.
- In order to utilize the principle, the term related with the /net working/ in $\frac{d}{dt} U[\Omega_t] = Q[\Omega_t] + \mathcal{W}[\Omega_t]$, needs to be worked out in terms of mechanical variables. 
** ($\S 5.3.4$) Characterization of Net Working
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- Here we derive a relation between the rate of change of kinetic energy and the power of external and internal forces.
- To begin, we take the dot product of the conservation of linear momentum equation with the spatial velocity field (from the right) and integrate the resulting equation over  $\mathcal{B}_t$
  $$ \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} \rho \dot{\boldsymbol{v}} \cdot \boldsymbol{v}\, dV = \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} [(\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}) \cdot \boldsymbol{v} + \rho \boldsymbol{b} \cdot \boldsymbol{v}]\, dV$$
- The term $(\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}) \cdot \boldsymbol{v}$ can be obtained from $\nabla \cdot (\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot \boldsymbol{v})$. Using this identity and by applying the divergence theorem, the above integral can  be converted to
  $$ \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} \rho \dot{\boldsymbol{v}} \cdot \boldsymbol{v}\, dV + \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} \boldsymbol{\sigma} : \nabla \boldsymbol{v} \, dV= \int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_t} \boldsymbol{n} \cdot (\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot \boldsymbol{v}) dA + \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} \rho \boldsymbol{b} \cdot \boldsymbol{v}\, dV$$
- Noting that $\boldsymbol{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} = \bar{\boldsymbol{t}}$ over the boundary $\partial\mathcal{B}$, we write the above expression as
  $$ \frac{d}{dt} \mathcal{K}[\mathcal{B}_t] + \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} \boldsymbol{\sigma} : \nabla\boldsymbol{v}\, dV = \mathcal{P}[\mathcal{B}_t].$$
- The term $\int_{\mathcal{B}_t} \boldsymbol{\sigma} : \nabla\boldsymbol{v}\, dV$ known as the *stress power* is identified as $\mathcal{W}[\mathcal{B}_t]$, in view of the definition of /net working/.
** ($\S 5.3.5$) The First Law in its Local Form
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- The balance of energy for the body $\mathcal{B}_t$ is
  $$ \frac{d}{dt} U[\Omega_t] = \mathcal{W}[\Omega_t] + Q[\Omega_t] $$
- Using the stress power obtained in the previous step for the net working, and the net heating which was previously derived in the above expression, we get
  $$ \frac{d}{dt} \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} \rho\phi\, dV = \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} \boldsymbol{\sigma} : \nabla\boldsymbol{v}\, dV - \int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_t} \boldsymbol{q} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}\, dA + \int_{\mathcal{B}} \rho r\, dV. $$
- Using the divergence theorem to convert the surface integral to a boundary integral, the above integral is converted to read
  $$ \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} \rho \dot{\phi}\, dV = \int_{\mathcal{B}_t} [\boldsymbol{\sigma} : \nabla\boldsymbol{v} - \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{q} + \rho r]\, dV. $$
- The localization principle applied to the balance of energy integral yields the same principle in its local form
  $$ \rho \dot{\phi} = \boldsymbol{\sigma} : \nabla\boldsymbol{v} - \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{q} + \rho r, \quad \forall \boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{B}_t,\; t\geq 0. $$
