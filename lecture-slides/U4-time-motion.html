(\(\S 4.4\)) Motions (Session 13)

  • In this chapter many important and advanced concepts such as deformation maps, strain, rate of deformation are introduced.
  • However, we will reserve our attention to velocity, the material time derivative, acceleration and vorticity only.
  • Even under this limitation, understanding of the material time derivative by itself is an important conceptualization effort.

(\(\S 4.4.4\)) Velocity and the Material Time Derivative

  • The spatial description of the material velocity field is denoted by \[ \boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t) \]
  • Let a continuum body \(\mathcal{B}\) be moving in a spatial scalar field \(\phi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\) with velocity \(\boldsymbol{v}\). Then the material time derivative of \(\phi\) is given by \[ \dot{\phi} = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t} + \boldsymbol{v} \cdot \nabla\phi \]
  • Proof follows from the fact that spatial field is a function of \(\boldsymbol{x}\), which in turn is a function of \(t\). Thus, the derivative has to be applied following the chain rule of differentiation.
  • The two terms have different physical implications:
    • \(\partial\phi/\partial t\) is called the local time derivative, while
    • \(\boldsymbol{v} \cdot \nabla\phi\) is called the convective derivative.
    • Note that the symbol \(\dot{\square}\) and \(\frac{\partial\square}{\partial t}\) have different outcomes.

Material Time Derivative of a Vector Field

  • Let a continuum body \(\mathcal{B}\) be moving in a spatial vector field \(\boldsymbol{b}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\) with velocity \(\boldsymbol{v}\). Then the material time derivative of \(\boldsymbol{b}\) is given by \[ \dot{\boldsymbol{b}} = \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{b}}{\partial t} + \boldsymbol{v} \cdot \nabla\boldsymbol{b} \]
  • Proof is similar to the one for scalar fields.

Acceleration as the Material Time Derivative of the Velocity Field

  • By definition, the spatial acceleration field \(\boldsymbol{a}\) of a moving body is \(\boldsymbol{a} = \dot{\boldsymbol{v}}\), where \(\boldsymbol{v}\) is the spatial velocity of the motion. From the previous concept of material time derivative, we deduce \[\boldsymbol{a} = \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}{\partial t} + \boldsymbol{v} \cdot (\nabla\boldsymbol{v}) .\]

Vorticity

  • By vorticity of a motion we mean the spatial vector field \[ \boldsymbol{w} = \nabla \times \boldsymbol{v}.\]
  • Vorticity is a measure of circulation in a flow.
