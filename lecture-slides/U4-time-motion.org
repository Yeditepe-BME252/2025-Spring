** ($\S 4.4.4$) Velocity and the Material Time Derivative
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- The spatial description of the material velocity field is denoted by
  $$ \boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t) $$
- Let a continuum body $\mathcal{B}$ be moving in a spatial scalar field $\phi(\boldsymbol{x},t)$ with velocity $\boldsymbol{v}$. Then the material time derivative of $\phi$ is given by
  $$ \dot{\phi} = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t} + \boldsymbol{v} \cdot \nabla\phi $$
- /Proof/ follows from the fact that spatial field is a function of $\boldsymbol{x}$, which in turn is a function of $t$. Thus, the derivative has to be applied following the chain rule of differentiation.
- The two terms have different physical implications:
  #+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
  + $\partial\phi/\partial t$ is called the /local time derivative/, while
  + $\boldsymbol{v} \cdot \nabla\phi$ is called the /convective derivative/.
  + Note that the symbol $\dot{\square}$ and $\frac{\partial\square}{\partial t}$ have different outcomes.

*** Material Time Derivative of a Vector Field
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear appear appear ...)
- Let a continuum body $\mathcal{B}$ be moving in a spatial vector field $\boldsymbol{b}(\boldsymbol{x},t)$ with velocity $\boldsymbol{v}$. Then the material time derivative of $\boldsymbol{b}$ is given by
  $$ \dot{\boldsymbol{b}} = \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{b}}{\partial t} + \boldsymbol{v} \cdot \nabla\boldsymbol{b} $$
- /Proof/ is similar to the one for scalar fields.

*** Acceleration as the Material Time Derivative of the Velocity Field
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- By definition, the spatial acceleration field $\boldsymbol{a}$ of a moving body is $\boldsymbol{a} = \dot{\boldsymbol{v}}$, where $\boldsymbol{v}$ is the spatial velocity of the motion. From the previous concept of material time derivative, we deduce
  $$\boldsymbol{a} = \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}{\partial t} + \boldsymbol{v} \cdot (\nabla\boldsymbol{v}) .$$

*** Vorticity
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- By vorticity of a motion we mean the spatial vector field
  $$ \boldsymbol{w} = \nabla \times \boldsymbol{v}.$$
- Vorticity is a measure of circulation in a flow.
